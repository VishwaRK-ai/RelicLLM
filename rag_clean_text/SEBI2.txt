Income Business Discretionary Essentials Emergency Interest Rent Salary Expenditure Expenditure refers to the money spent to meet daily needs and financial obligations. It includes essential expenses such as food, housing, education, transport, healthcare and utility bills, as well as non-essential or discretionary expenses like shopping, dining out, or entertainment. Understanding and tracking expenditure is key to budgeting. A balanced budget ensures that expenses do not exceed income. "Spend less than you earn." Regular review of expenses helps cut down wasteful spending and improve savings. Income is the total amount of money earned by an individual or household from all sources such as salary, business, rent, interest, pension or government schemes. It is the foundation of personal finance. Managing income starts with understanding how much is earned, how regularly it is received and from where. To achieve financial security, it is important to not only earn but also diversify income sources over time—for example, by developing skills, taking up part-time work, or investing in income-generating assets. Savings Savings is the portion of income that is not spent and is kept aside for future use. It acts as a financial cushion during emergencies and helps meet short- and long-term goals such as buying a house, funding education or planning retirement. Building the habit of saving—no matter how small the amount—is the first step toward financial independence. Ideally, one should save at least 20% of monthly income and maintain an emergency fund equal to 6 months of expenses. Savings should be safe, easily accessible, and ideally kept in a bank account or liquid assets. Saving is like planting a seed. The more you care, the bigger the tree grows. 1.Income and Saving 5

Investment Options 🏦 1. Bank Fixed Deposits (FDs) Bank FDs are one of the safest and most traditional investment options. You deposit a fixed amount with a bank for a fixed period (e.g., 7 days to 10 years). You earn interest at a pre-decided and guaranteed rate. Ideal for conservative investors seeking stable and fixed returns. As per DICGC rules, all deposits in Indian banks are insured up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in any unforeseen event. 📄 2. Public Provident Fund (PPF) The PPF is a government-backed savings scheme that offers attractive interest and complete safety. It has a lock-in period of 15 years, but the interest earned is tax- free. You can invest as little as ₹500 and up to ₹1.5 lakh per year. PPF is suited for long-term goals like retirement and children's education. The power of compounding over time helps build a significant corpus without any market risk. You can make one partial withdrawal per year from your PPF account after 5 financial years from the date of opening the account. 3. Mutual Funds Asset Management Company (Mutual Fund Houses) pools money from investors and invest in a diversified portfolio of stocks, bonds or hybrid. Mutual Funds are managed by professional fund managers. Mutual funds offer easy entry, liquidity, and diversification. They are ideal for investors who seek better long-term returns than savings or fixed deposits (FDs) without the hassle of selecting individual stocks. 4. Stocks (Equity Shares) Investing in stocks means buying ownership in publicly listed companies. When the company performs well, its stock price increases and you can earn capital gain. Stocks may also pay dividends. Stock investments carry higher risk due to market fluctuations, but they offer the potential for high returns in the long term. Stocks are suitable for informed investors with a good risk appetite and a long investment horizon. 📈 8 

Gold has been a preferred investment for generations. Today, apart from physical gold (jewelry, coins), investors can choose safer and more convenient options like Gold ETFs. Gold protects against inflation in the long run and acts as a financial safety net during uncertain times. While gold prices may fluctuate in the short term, it is considered a stable long-term asset. The National Pension System (NPS), regulated by PFRDA, is a government-backed retirement scheme allowing individuals to invest regularly during working years and receive pensions and lump sum after retirement. The money is invested in equity, corporate bonds and government securities, depending on the investor’s age and choice. It offers tax benefits and partial withdrawal after a few years. NPS is suitable for disciplined retirement planning. Bonds and debentures are debt instruments where you lend money to a government or company for a fixed tenure and earn regular interest. They are relatively low-risk compared to equity and provide predictable income. Government bonds are safer, while corporate bonds offer higher returns with a slightly higher risk. Investment Options Post Office investment schemes are ideal for small savers and are backed by the Government of India. Popular schemes include the Monthly Income Scheme (MIS), Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS), National Savings Scheme and Kisan Vikas Patra. These schemes offer fixed returns, are safe, and promote savings among rural and semi-urban populations. 6. Gold 7. National Pension System (NPS) 🧓 9. Post Office Schemes 🧾 10.
